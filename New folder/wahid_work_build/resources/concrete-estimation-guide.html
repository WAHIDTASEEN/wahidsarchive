CONSTRUCTION ESTIMATION GUIDE

Concrete Material Estimation: A Practical Starting Method

Understand how preliminary cement, sand and aggregate estimates can be made from concrete volume and a nominal mix ratio.

Why dry volume is used

Fresh concrete contains voids and materials become rearranged during mixing. A preliminary estimation therefore converts wet concrete volume into an assumed dry-material volume using a factor selected by the method being taught.

Dry material volume ≈ Wet concrete volume × dry-volume factor

A commonly used classroom factor is 1.54. It should be treated as a method-specific estimating assumption rather than a universal design constant.

Nominal mix ratios

For a nominal proportion such as 1 : 1.5 : 3, the parts represent cement : sand : coarse aggregate by volume. The total proportion is 5.5 parts.

Cement share = 1 / 5.5
Sand share = 1.5 / 5.5
Aggregate share = 3 / 5.5

Cement bags

After finding the cement volume, the volume is converted to mass using the density assumption adopted by the estimating method, then divided by the assumed bag mass. Because bag yield and density assumptions vary, the calculation should clearly state its assumptions.

What this estimate does not replace

  • Concrete mix design based on specified strength and workability.
  • Trial mixes and laboratory testing.
  • Specification requirements for cement, aggregate and water.
  • Site-specific wastage and batching conditions.
Use a nominal-mix estimator for learning and preliminary quantity checks. For structural concrete, use the approved mix design and project specification rather than treating an online estimate as a construction instruction.
