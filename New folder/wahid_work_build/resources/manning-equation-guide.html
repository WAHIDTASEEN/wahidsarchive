Manning’s Equation: A Practical Civil Engineering Guide

Understand the variables in Manning’s equation, how hydraulic radius is used, and how to check an open-channel discharge calculation.

What the equation does

Manning’s equation estimates the mean flow velocity or discharge in an open channel when the channel roughness, hydraulic radius and energy slope are known. It is commonly used for preliminary open-channel hydraulics calculations.

V = (1 / n) R^(2/3) S^(1/2)
Q = A V

Meaning of the variables

  • Q is discharge, usually in m³/s.
  • V is mean velocity, usually in m/s.
  • n is Manning’s roughness coefficient.
  • A is flow area in m².
  • R is hydraulic radius, A/P, where P is the wetted perimeter.
  • S is the energy slope used in the Manning calculation; for uniform flow it is commonly approximated by the channel bed slope.

A reliable calculation sequence

  1. Determine the flow area A from the channel geometry.
  2. Determine the wetted perimeter P.
  3. Calculate hydraulic radius R = A/P.
  4. Select a defensible roughness coefficient from the course manual or design reference.
  5. Use the slope as a dimensionless ratio, not a percentage unless converted correctly.
  6. Calculate velocity and then Q = AV.
Always check units and the selected roughness value against the laboratory manual, design specification or applicable standard. A calculator can perform the arithmetic, but it cannot choose an appropriate roughness coefficient for your project.

Common mistakes

  • Using hydraulic depth instead of hydraulic radius.
  • Entering slope as 0.1 when the intended slope is 0.1% and should be 0.001.
  • Mixing centimetres or millimetres with metre-based quantities.
  • Using a roughness coefficient without checking whether it matches the channel surface.
